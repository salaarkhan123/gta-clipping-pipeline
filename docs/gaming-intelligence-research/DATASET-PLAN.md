# Gaming highlight dataset

The metadata pilot identified **116 higher-reach uploads and 116 matched lower-reach uploads** across nine game families. The next study needs the underlying, permissioned footage, creator analytics at a common publication age, and ordinary passages from the same recording sessions. These serve different purposes. A published clip has exposure and an editorial decision behind it. A random passage of a VOD has neither. [Pilot, selection code and observed results](VIRALITY-RESEARCH.md)

## Sampling and annotation

1. **Recruit and preregister.** Target **220 successful published clips** (20 per requested game/category), one lower-performing published control per case and one randomly located ordinary window per case. Budget permits a first wave of 110 positives with 110 controls of each kind. Recruit at least four independent creators per category and cap any one creator at 25% within a category. If a stratum has insufficient consent or exposure, report its actual size and withhold game-specific conclusions. Balance small and large channels, platform, language, facecam/chat presence, VOD lengths, resolution, reuploads and editing styles.
2. **Select posts from an enumerated frame.** Ask for the complete eligible posting history during fixed calendar intervals plus pre-publication creator size, age-indexed exposure/retention measures and format. Assess success within game, creator or comparable size band, platform, format and fixed publication age. Prespecify top and bottom quartiles of day-7 retention relative to same-duration peers, conditional on an exposure floor set on pilot data. Use a separate reach outcome for cases with exposure but no retention. Match nearest prior/post dates within 30 days and length within 10 s where possible. Report unmatched rows and missing analytics. Never assign missing engagement a zero.
3. **Add ordinary controls.** Draw random playable start times in a creator's permissioned source VOD using a recorded seed. Match duration, title, layout and session to a positive. Retain menus, replay, spectator and routine combat as labeled negative strata. Annotate each draw blind first and keep incidental positives as such; continue drawing to meet the ordinary quota. Unpublished windows receive no engagement outcome.
4. **Keep source and publication separate.** Link the published cut to VOD session, original interval, central event, reaction and clip end. A source VOD does not contain added captions, crop, pace edits or end card by default. Identify each feature as observable in source, publication or owner analytics. Track replay, duplicate montages, reaction rehosts and multiple cuts of a single play as one source family.
5. **Label independently.** Two reviewers familiar with the title see masked identity where practical and no counts, competitor or model scores. Mark context start, event onset, payoff and safe exit in source and edited cut, plus 1–3 postability, reason and uncertainty. Adjudicate event identity and disagreements above 2 s on payoff or one rating level. Report weighted kappa for ratings, median absolute time disagreement, and repeat 10% for rater drift. Human reviewers separately tag facecam availability and visible expressive movement without assigning internal emotions.
6. **Partition by family.** Freeze creator-disjoint development, calibration and final test groups at approximately 60/20/20, preserving games where possible. Hold a new game or patch out for transfer tests, and run chronological validation on development creators. Publishers, co-streamers, the same match/round, reposts and near duplicates travel together. Reserve calibration for operating points and per-game thresholds. Keep an independent untouched set for reporting. Counts may differ from target after grouping.
7. **Protect existing evaluation.** Treat all v1/v2 source families, their existing candidate sheets, runs, annotations and competitor exports as evaluation only. Explicitly reserve the 2h GTA benchmark, reaction benchmark, GTA RP holdout, plus the local GTA V, GTA VI, GTA Online, Fortnite, Valorant and Forza fixtures and their variants. Maintain a private denylist of source IDs/URLs, session IDs, content hashes, audio fingerprints and frame hashes. Match across platform IDs and near-duplicate intervals before any annotation for training, including montage excerpts. Escalate ambiguous aliases to a reviewer and quarantine unresolved rows. The historical pilot contains metadata only and never enters training. The checked-in evaluation inventory contains no published watch URLs to match directly against these IDs. Public IDs alone cannot prove disjointness against private footage. [Existing evaluation](../../clip-api/eval/README.md)

## Minimum row and release gate

Each private row needs sample ID, creator pseudonym, event-family ID, license/consent record and expiry, source platform and ID, game/patch/layout, language, media resolution and timeline timebase, publication age and format, selection probability or source census, case/control type, measured exposure, metric definition, missing flags, feature observability, independent ratings, boundaries, split and deduplication status. Use numeric source times, preserve raw metric timestamps, and keep real handles, chat text, video files and export links outside Git.

Release only pseudonymous aggregates and a reproducible selector under the rights granted for each source. Accept the study for modeling once there are at least 100 permissioned successes, matched poor-performing posts and independently labeled ordinary passages, represented creators and split sizes reported, zero unresolved cross-split/evaluation aliases among admitted rows, and a deliberate decision per use for all footage. Selection into the existing 116-pair reach pilot alone cannot satisfy those conditions.
